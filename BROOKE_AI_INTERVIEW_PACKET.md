# Brooke AI Interview Packet

Brooke, this packet is meant to help you think out loud with your AI.

You do not need polished answers. Short, honest, imperfect answers are better than trying to sound finished. The goal is to separate what is already true, what is still a dream, what needs a decision, and what should not be promised publicly yet.

You do not need access to any website repo, GitHub account, or technical workspace. This file is meant to be uploaded directly into Claude as an attachment.

## How To Use This

1. Open Claude.
2. Start a new chat.
3. Attach this Markdown file.
4. Paste the prompt below.
5. Answer Claude one section at a time.
6. When Claude gives you the final summary, send that summary back to Josue.

```text
You are helping me clarify the next business decisions for Cultivate.

Use the attached Brooke AI Interview Packet as your guide.

Please interview me one section at a time. Do not ask every question at once. Start with the most important question, wait for my answer, ask follow-up questions only when needed, and keep the process calm and manageable.

For each section, help me produce:

1. My current answer in plain language.
2. What can be said publicly.
3. What should stay private for now.
4. What still needs proof, pricing, scheduling, legal review, partner confirmation, or operational validation.
5. A short note I can send back to Josue for the website.

When we finish, create a final summary with these headings:

- Approved Direction
- Still Private
- Needs Validation
- Website Updates For Josue
- Open Questions For Later

Keep your tone direct, warm, and practical. Help me decide; do not flatter me or turn this into a branding exercise.
```

## The Main Goal

The next round of work is not to solve every future version of Cultivate.

The next round is to answer enough for Josue to turn the private preview into a clearer owner-approved direction, then eventually into a public-facing first version.

Focus on:

- what Cultivate is right now
- what the first public offer should be
- which parts of the bigger ecosystem are real enough to describe
- which parts should remain future vision
- what families, partners, and supporters can safely be told

## Section 1: What Cultivate Is

Start here. This affects every page.

1. If someone asks, "What is Cultivate?", what do you want the simplest honest answer to be?
2. Which description feels closest right now?
   - a learning community
   - a homeschool enrichment space
   - a microschool-adjacent ecosystem
   - a village for families
   - a teen growth and contribution model
   - something else
3. What should Cultivate not be mistaken for?
4. What words feel wrong, too formal, too clinical, too school-like, or too vague?
5. What feeling should a parent have after reading the first page?

Decision needed:

- The public category line for Cultivate.

Output for Josue:

- "Cultivate should be described as..."
- "Avoid calling it..."

## Section 2: Brooke's Story

This does not need to become a long founder bio. It just needs enough truth to make the concept feel human.

1. Why does Cultivate exist for you personally?
2. What problem did you see that made this feel necessary?
3. What part of your story are you comfortable sharing publicly?
4. What part of your story should stay private?
5. What do you want people to trust about your motivation?

Decision needed:

- The founder story boundary.

Output for Josue:

- "The public story can say..."
- "Do not include..."

## Section 3: The First Offer

This is the most important business section. The first public version should be clear, even if the full vision is bigger.

1. What is the first thing Cultivate should actually invite families into?
2. Who is it for first?
   - age range
   - family type
   - learner needs
   - parent needs
3. What is the first realistic format?
   - weekly gathering
   - cohort
   - workshop series
   - membership
   - drop-in experience
   - pilot program
4. How many families or learners could the first version responsibly serve?
5. What would a family get from participating?
6. What should families not expect yet?

Decision needed:

- The first public offer.

Output for Josue:

- "The first offer is..."
- "It is for..."
- "It is not yet..."

## Section 4: Schedule, Place, And Capacity

Only answer what is realistic. It is okay to say "not ready yet."

1. Where could the first version happen?
2. How often could it happen?
3. What time of day or week seems most realistic?
4. What capacity feels responsible for a first version?
5. What needs to be confirmed before any schedule or location is public?

Decision needed:

- Whether the website can mention timing, location, or capacity.

Output for Josue:

- "Public copy may mention..."
- "Public copy should not mention..."

## Section 5: Pricing And Support

This can stay general if exact numbers are not ready.

1. Should Cultivate be framed as paid, donation-supported, scholarship-supported, membership-based, or still undecided?
2. Are there any price ranges you are comfortable exploring privately?
3. Should public copy mention affordability, scholarships, or founding-family pricing?
4. What should not be promised yet?

Decision needed:

- Whether pricing appears publicly now, later, or not at all yet.

Output for Josue:

- "Pricing should be handled as..."
- "Do not promise..."

## Section 6: Spaces And Ecosystem Pieces

The preview includes the full ecosystem. This section decides what is day-one, future-facing, or private planning only.

For each piece, choose one status:

- Day-one
- Possible early phase
- Future vision
- Private planning only
- Remove or rename

Pieces to review:

- The Glade
- The Sprout Space
- The Grove
- The Commons
- The Harvest Cafe
- Foxtail partnership
- The Stepping Stones
- The Wilds outdoor campus

For each one, answer:

1. What is real enough to say now?
2. What is still aspirational?
3. What would need to be true before this becomes public?

Decision needed:

- Which ecosystem pieces can be publicly visible.

Output for Josue:

- "Public now..."
- "Future vision..."
- "Private only..."
- "Rename/remove..."

## Section 7: Higher-Risk Promises

These areas need careful language.

1. Can Cultivate publicly mention childcare or child supervision?
2. Can Cultivate publicly mention cafe service, food, or beverage sales?
3. Can Cultivate publicly mention rentals, coworking, or drop-in access?
4. Can Cultivate publicly mention paid teen work, teen responsibility, or apprenticeships?
5. Can Cultivate publicly mention Foxtail?
6. Can Cultivate publicly mention The Wilds?

For each, choose:

- yes, approved
- yes, but only with careful wording
- not yet
- no

Decision needed:

- Promise boundaries.

Output for Josue:

- "Approved language..."
- "Careful language..."
- "Do not mention yet..."

## Section 8: Trust, Safety, And Proof

This helps the public version feel credible without overpromising.

1. What proof can be used?

   - testimonials
   - partner names
   - credentials
   - local references
   - parent quotes
   - pilot results
2. What safety or supervision details are ready to share?
3. What permissions are needed before anything is published?
4. What should the website say while proof is still being gathered?

Decision needed:

- Which trust assets are usable now.

Output for Josue:

- "Approved proof..."
- "Need permission..."
- "Placeholder only..."

## Section 9: Public Call To Action

This determines what the first public page asks people to do.

1. What should an interested parent do first?
   - email Brooke
   - join an interest list
   - request a conversation
   - apply for a first cohort
   - attend an info session
   - wait for updates
2. What information should the first form collect?
3. Who receives the inquiry?
4. How fast should someone expect a response?
5. What privacy language feels necessary?

Decision needed:

- The first website call to action.

Output for Josue:

- "The CTA should be..."
- "The form should ask..."
- "The follow-up should be..."

## Section 10: What Brooke Wants To See Next

This keeps the next build round practical.

1. What part of the current preview feels most right?
2. What part feels off, too big, too vague, or not yours?
3. What would make you feel proud to show this to one trusted person?
4. What would make you uncomfortable if it were public tomorrow?
5. What should Josue prioritize next?

Decision needed:

- The next build pass.

Output for Josue:

- "Keep..."
- "Change..."
- "Remove..."
- "Build next..."

## A Good Answer Can Be Short

Example:

```text
Cultivate should publicly be described as a learning community for families who want more connection, rhythm, and real-world growth than traditional options are giving them.

Do not call it a school yet.

The first offer should be a small founding-family cohort. The cafe, rentals, Foxtail, and Wilds should stay future-facing until details are confirmed.
```

That is enough to move the website forward.
